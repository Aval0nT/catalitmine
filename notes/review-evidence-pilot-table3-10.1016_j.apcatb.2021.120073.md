## Table-level context extracted from text
The review states around Table 3 that:
- Cu-based materials are efficient catalysts for methanol synthesis and RWGS.
- Table 3 summarizes representative Cu-based catalysts for CO2 conversion to higher alcohols.
- Typical reaction conditions are around:
  - `300–350 °C`
  - `3.0–8.0 MPa`
  - `3600–20,000 h^-1`
  - `H2/CO2 ratio ~3.0`
- Cu-based materials promoted by `Zn, Fe, K, Pd, Ga, and Co` are discussed as potential catalysts for higher alcohol synthesis from CO2.
- For most catalysts in the table, `(CO + hydrocarbons)` selectivity remains high.
- Methanol selectivity is below `18%`.
- `C2+ alcohol selectivity` ranges from `1.3%` to `31.9%`.
- `C2+OH yield` ranges from `0.36%` to `13.51%`.
- The review text states that a `CuZnFeK` catalyst gives the best results among the tabulated Cu-based gas-phase catalysts.
- A slurry batch system using `Zr12-bpdc-CuCs` in THF at `<100 °C` is highlighted for very high ethanol selectivity (`99%`) and high yield (`40.04%`).

## First-pass patterns visible from this table alone

### Pattern 1 — Cu alone is not enough
Rows such as `CuZnK` are much weaker for higher alcohol output than `CuZnFeK`, suggesting that Cu-centered systems require additional compositional tuning to move beyond CO/methanol-dominant behavior.

### Pattern 2 — Fe appears repeatedly in stronger Cu entries
The stronger gas-phase Cu rows repeatedly include `Fe` together with `Zn` and `K`, suggesting Fe may be a key co-component for improving C2+ alcohol formation.

### Pattern 3 — K is common but not sufficient
K promotion is frequent across Cu rows, but performance varies widely. That implies K is likely necessary in many reported systems but not by itself predictive of high higher-alcohol yield.

### Pattern 4 — Gas-phase vs slurry batch may define different performance regimes
`Zr12-bpdc-CuCs` in THF slurry batch appears qualitatively different from the gas-phase fixed-bed Cu systems and should probably not be mixed into the same benchmark pool without a reactor-mode flag.

## Immediate follow-up questions generated by this table
1. Is `Fe` the most important co-component in successful Cu-based gas-phase HAS systems?
2. Does `Zn + Fe + K + Cu` form a recurring high-value motif across multiple references?
3. Should slurry-batch MOF-supported Cu/Cs systems be treated as a separate class from conventional fixed-bed Cu catalysts?
4. Are the strong CuZnFeK results mechanistically tied to CO-mediated coupling rather than methanol-mediated pathways?
5. Which Cu-based systems were **not** tested with the strongest-looking promoter/co-metal combinations?
